# v0.2 targeted literature and claims map (proposal)

Checked 16 November 2026 against original papers or institutional records. This is a targeted source check for study design, not a systematic review. A cited result applies only to its own population, motion model and site. No OpenYield performance or novelty claim follows from this map.

| Source and original link | What the source actually studies | Design use here | Boundary on claims |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Wang, Avedisov, Altintas & Orosz (2024), *Negotiation in Cooperative Maneuvering using Conflict Analysis*](https://websites.umich.edu/~orosz/articles/2024_IVS_Wang_Avedisov_Altintas_Orosz.pdf) | Request/response feasibility and V2X experiments for an unsignalized intersection; compares negotiation with status/intent sharing and examines delay. | Separate requests, local feasibility checks, agreement and communication overhead in later v0.3 design. | Intersection results do not establish narrow-road gains; v0.2 has no negotiation. Their assumptions and vehicle dynamics differ from our axis-aligned rectangles. |
| [Rettenmaier & Bengler (2021), *The Matter of How and When*](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1738468/1738468.pdf) | Driving-simulator study with 31 human participants, an AV, explicit display and motion cues at bottlenecks; passing time is measured over a declared spatial window. | Predeclare start/end markers and completion-time definitions; recognize motion as a possible signal. | Human comprehension and simulator passing-time effects are not AV–AV communication effects. The paper's offset develops over distance; it does not validate instantaneous lateral translation. This is a plausible match to the brief's ambiguous “Rettenmaier and Bengler bottleneck communication” lead. |
| [Rettenmaier, Dinkel & Bengler (2021), *Communication via motion*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0003687021000855) | Human interpretation of an automated vehicle's movements in bottleneck scenarios. | Context for future implicit signaling questions. | No evidence that our abstract bay movement is physically or socially valid. |
| [Quante, Gimm & Schießl (2023), *Trajectory-based traffic observation of cooperation at a road narrowing*](https://elib.dlr.de/194714/) | Observational analysis of 209 human encounters at one two-sided narrowing; at that location, direction mattered more than arrival order for passing order. | Include a fixed direction-priority comparator and swapped approach cases; do not assume first arrival is universally normative. | Site-specific human behavior is not a general AV rule or evidence of an OpenYield effect. |
| [de Oliveira, *Analyzing the performance of distributed conflict resolution among autonomous vehicles*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08127) | Network traffic game with distributed priority-based resource allocation; studies cooperation, collective cost and equity under its model. | Treat throughput and fairness as separate outcomes and state allocation rules precisely. | A network routing/resource model does not validate a two-car passing-bay controller. The arXiv posting is dated 2021; the older publication/year lead remains unreconciled. |
| [Cao & Zhao (2022), journal record and abstract](https://www.zjujournals.com/eng/EN/10.3785/j.issn.1008-973X.2022.01.013) | The available abstract describes pedestrian–vehicle right of way despite a broad road-segment title. | Bibliographic context only. | Do not present as a narrow-road AV–AV comparator without inspecting a full original text. |

## Claims allowed at this stage

| Proposed wording | Evidence | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “OpenYield v0.1 is a deterministic scripted geometry demonstration with a global fail-stop monitor.” | `src/openyield/simulation.py`, `monitor.py`, `docs/model.md`; merged [PR #2](https://github.com/openyield-initiative/openyield/pull/2). | Supported by repository inspection; not a safety certification. |
| “Passing order, communication and fairness are distinct design variables worth testing.” | Wang et al. for negotiation, Quante et al. for site-dependent priority, de Oliveira for equity/cost under another model. | Motivation, not a result for OpenYield. |
| “Negotiation improves narrow-road efficiency/reliability over these baselines.” | Requires controlled OpenYield experiments after v0.3 and robustness analysis after v0.4. | **Not established; do not claim.** |
| “First arrival is the fair or correct rule.” | Quante et al. found a different prevailing rule at one observed location. | **Not established.** First arrival is a comparator, not a normative standard. |
| “The model represents feasible car steering or safe deployment.” | Current motion permits fixed-heading lateral translation and instantaneous speed changes. | **False for current model; do not claim.** |

Before any manuscript or novelty statement, reconcile the de Oliveira publication record, inspect full methods of any newly cited comparator, and expand the search to closer AV–AV passing-bay work. The present map is deliberately proportional to a v0.2 baseline design.
