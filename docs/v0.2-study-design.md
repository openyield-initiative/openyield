# v0.2 baselines and research design — proposed for owner review

Status: **design only**, 16 November 2026. This document proposes an abstract, deterministic comparison. Controller implementation, results, release and publication need separate approval. [Targeted sources and claims map](v0.2-literature-and-claims.md).

## Intent, scope and model decision

Question: under a common narrow-road geometry and motion model, how do independent local yielding, fixed direction priority and first arrival behave before a v0.3 negotiation policy is added? v0.2 should establish reproducible baselines and measurement, not demonstrate an advantage for negotiation.

The existing demonstration starts A **already in the bay**, hardcodes the two destinations, and lets A slide sideways with fixed heading. It cannot fairly rank strategies when bay access and yielding are the actual decisions. The least disruptive upgrade is to retain the axis-aligned, straight-segment translation model as an **abstract space-allocation simulator**, start both vehicles on their approach lanes outside the bay, give both the same geometry/goal schema and motion primitives, and label results as abstract. Each action is wait or a bounded straight translation in x **or** y, with distance at most configured speed × `dt`; no teleporting, diagonal movement, reverse travel or instant change of footprint/heading. Speed changes remain instantaneous. Before implementation, a geometric feasibility check must show that *each* vehicle can reach a safe bay holding pose and its destination using those primitives without moving the other vehicle. Exclude an infeasible geometry rather than treating it as policy failure.

Alternatives considered: (1) compare policies on the current already-in-bay script, which confounds the choice of yielding with an initial advantage; (2) use the proposed abstract motion upgrade, which enables a controlled baseline but has no steering realism; (3) introduce a kinematic bicycle/steering model now, which would require new path planning and validation before the comparison. **Proposed choice: (2)**. Owner approval would approve this study model, not a realism claim. SUMO, ROS and learned policies add no necessary capability for this stage.

## Common comparison contract

Each scenario file fixes road, obstacles, one or more marked passing bays, footprints, initial poses, constant maximum translation speeds, clearance, `dt`, duration, and **controller-independent goal centre poses** on opposite sides. A vehicle completes when its centre reaches its own specified `(x,y)` goal and it stops; a commanded move may clamp to that goal within the speed cap. Completion is checked after the external monitor accepts the step. Goals and input validation are identical for every policy. Any scenario-specific control parameter is in the common manifest and is visible to all policies.

The simulator calls two separate controller instances each tick. Both receive the same static map, own pose/goal, and the other vehicle's **currently visible pose, heading and speed cap**. v0.2 assumes perfect simultaneous observation over the entire road, no occlusion/noise, identical clocks and zero sensing latency. This is a declared abstraction, not privileged hidden intent. A controller sees no other controller memory, proposed action, future path or global monitor result. It may retain only its own previous observations/decisions. Both choose from the same movement primitives and use the same **local** geometric action filter: reject a candidate if its own swept footprint fails road/obstacle clearance or intersects any one-step sweep of the other car's observed footprint under wait or cardinal translation of up to that car's speed cap × `dt`, expanded by clearance; then choose wait. The filter uses the union of these possible sweeps, including travel directions the other policy might never select, and may be conservative. Candidate action order is fixed in tests before results. No policy can call the global joint-state monitor as a planner.

The external simulator separately checks the **joint continuous swept motion** of both proposed actions using the existing monitor and fail-stops on an unsafe proposal. That monitor has both simultaneous proposed actions, information individual controllers do not have. A zero global rejection count is an audit outcome, not proof of local safety under real sensing/dynamics. If a local filter is too conservative for progress, record it as such; do not selectively loosen it for a baseline. Same accepted-action speed bound, observation timing, goal check, `dt`, duration and tie rules apply to all policies. For a future negotiation comparison, messages and any communication latency/overhead are an explicit **treatment information advantage**; physical observations and action filters stay fixed. A separate information-matched ablation may be needed to isolate agreement from extra information.

### Precisely defined policy candidates

All policies share a deterministic waypoint generator that can find a safe approach, bay hold, re-entry and goal route in empty space. The generator uses only static map, own goal and current observations, never an assignment of who yields. If either side has no such route, the scenario is marked infeasible before comparison.

1. **Independent local yielding (communication-free):** each vehicle prefers its direct approach route while the other is absent from its one-step conflict envelope. When an encounter is predicted, it independently chooses `yield` if it can reach a safe bay holding pose without conflicting with the observed car; otherwise it waits at a safe approach stop. If both choose yield, both may wait or enter different holds; no identity-based winner, shared intention, message, synchronized hidden variable or global arbitration is available. Once the other is observed to have cleared the conflict region, it resumes. This intentionally simple reactive comparator may deadlock; label it as such. To avoid an accidental implicit priority, candidate tie order must be symmetric under swapping A/B and east/west. If geometry itself breaks symmetry, record that asymmetry.
2. **Fixed direction priority (communication-free rule):** eastbound has priority in the primary condition. Westbound yields at a safe bay pose or approach stop; eastbound proceeds only when its local filter admits a step. After eastbound has visibly cleared the conflict region, westbound resumes. Also run the **reversed priority** condition for bias analysis. Both vehicles know the same public rule and observe the same map; no central agent assigns a winner at runtime.
3. **First arrival (communication-free rule under this observation assumption):** define an approach gate for each direction outside the bottleneck. The first vehicle **observed crossing its own gate** earns priority; both can observe both gates and persist that crossing event in local history. A same-tick tie goes to eastbound by a published rule, with a mirrored tie run to expose bias. Before either crosses, each follows the common local filter and may stop; after crossing, the losing vehicle yields as above. Gate locations and initial poses must make both crossing events observable and feasible. If either controller's local arrival record disagrees, treat this as a protocol/observation failure, not quietly use a global timestamp. A simulator-computed first-arrival winner would be an **oracle comparator**, not this communication-free policy; do not call it independent.

If local safety based on a reachable worst-case envelope prevents both vehicles crossing approach gates in a candidate scenario, that is a useful deadlock result; it must not be hidden by accessing proposed joint actions. A separate permissive filter can be studied only as a labeled sensitivity condition applied to every policy.

## Outcomes and metrics (predeclared)

At the first reached condition, classify exactly one terminal outcome: `success` (both goal footprints reached), `collision_rejection`, `clearance_rejection`, `boundary_or_invalid_rejection`, `deadlock`, or `timeout`. The global monitor rejects the *whole* proposed step; accepted frames remain unchanged. If one rejected step has multiple violations, store all reasons and apply a deterministic classification precedence (physical contact > clearance > boundary/invalid), with raw reasons retained. A collision rejection is a **predicted/model contact in a proposed step**, not an executed crash. Exclude invalid initial or geometrically infeasible scenarios from outcome denominators and report them separately.

`deadlock` means neither unfinished vehicle moved and neither local controller changed its declared role/phase for 5.0 simulated seconds. This includes a policy refusing an available move; record local admissible actions as a diagnostic, not a condition for calling a stall. This is checked before timeout. Slow but nonzero progress, looping and oscillation remain `timeout` if unfinished at the common horizon. If the horizon is shorter than the 5.0-second test, deadlock cannot be diagnosed and is reported as timeout. `timeout` means no earlier terminal condition by `floor(max_duration/dt)` accepted steps. A single finished car waiting for an unfinished car is not deadlock unless the unfinished car also remains stationary and phase-stable for the window.

For each scenario-policy pair record success indicator, terminal reason, time-to-first rejection/deadlock/timeout, accepted steps, each vehicle's goal time `t_i` (undefined if unfinished), and joint completion time `T=max(t_A,t_B)` on successes. Define solo reference `t_i^solo` by running the same vehicle alone, same map/start/goal/speed/filter and `dt`, on its shortest admissible main-lane route; report both reference values with the manifest. Individual delay is `d_i=t_i-t_i^solo` only when that vehicle completes; joint extra time is `T-max(t_A^solo,t_B^solo)` on successes. Do not substitute the deadline for missing goal times or average successful times alone as if failures disappeared. Report the success fraction and the distribution of completion times **conditional on success**, with failure counts beside it. For a deterministic suite, these are descriptive counts, not statistical estimates.

Fairness within a successful run: `|d_A-d_B|` and `max(d_A,d_B)`, with direction and priority assignment recorded. Across matched mirrored cases, show eastbound/westbound mean delay and success counts separately; do not interpret one encounter's delay gap as a social fairness guarantee. Count waits, bay entries, local filter rejections by cause, external monitor rejections, and path length as diagnostics. A completed vehicle may have zero delay; negative measured delay requires inspecting the solo reference or route definition.

## Minimum reproducible scenario suite

Version each geometry and case in a manifest; use fixed numeric values, validation and a saved content hash. Proposed starting family, in metres: road `x=[0,30], y=[1,8]`; upper obstacles `x=[2,8]` and `[18,28]`, both `y=[3.5,7.5]`; passing-bay label `x=[9,15], y=[4.5,7.5]`; each vehicle has length `4`, width `1.5`, and centreline `y=2.5`. A starts eastbound at `(4,2.5)` and aims for `(26,2.5)`; B starts westbound at `(26,2.5)` and aims for `(4,2.5)`. Bay hold centre is initially `(12,6)`; its vehicle footprint is inside the road and clear of upper obstacles. Clearance `0.25`, `dt=0.25`, duration `80`. Approach gates are centre `x=9` for A and `x=15` for B; a crossing is the first accepted step at or beyond the gate in the travel direction. These numbers are **candidates for hand-checked feasibility before freeze**, not pre-approved valid experimental data. Cases share this road family and target markers, varying one declared factor at a time. A paired mirrored case swaps positions, speeds and direction labels where geometry permits; do not pretend an asymmetric road has a perfect mirror.

| Case | Purpose and required construction | Expected check (not a claimed result) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| G0 solo A / solo B | One vehicle at a time on each route. | Feasible route and `t_i^solo` for delay denominator. |
| E1 separated arrival | Starting family, A/B speed `2/2`; nominal no-wait gate times `2.5/5.5` seconds. | Each policy terminates reproducibly; priority and observed crossing logs agree with rule. |
| E2 reversed arrival | Starting family, A/B speed `1/3`; nominal gate times `5/3.75` seconds (discrete crossing at `5/3.75`). | Tests fixed priority against arrival order without changing controller affordances. |
| T1 exact gate tie | Starting family, A/B speed `1/2.2`; nominal gate times both `5` seconds. | First-arrival tie resolves to eastbound; mirrored tie resolves opposite label. |
| A1 asymmetric speeds | Starting family, A/B speed `1.5/2.5`; compare with E1. | Exposes whether priority delays are due to travel-time asymmetry. |
| A2 asymmetric bay access | Starting family except shift bay label to `x=[11,17]`, hold centre `(14,6)`, with obstacle geometry unchanged; compare with E1. | Tests geometry dependence and logs which vehicle can safely yield. |
| D1 symmetric conflict | Make upper obstacles `x=[2,8]` and `[22,28]`, bay `x=[12,18]`, hold `(15,6)`, gates `x=10/20`; A/B speed `2/2`, nominal gate time `3/3` seconds. | Reproduces a documented deadlock or safe resolution under the chosen filter; no forced success expectation. |
| X1 horizon | E1 geometry and speeds but `max_duration=3` seconds. | `timeout` distinct from deadlock; no 5-second stall can be labeled deadlock. |

Do not select cases based on which policy looks best. If E1/E2/T1 are impossible with the current single bay and stop geometry, revise geometry and document the revision before running comparisons. Use the same suite and parameters for all policies. v0.2 runs are deterministic; `seed` is recorded but unused until a future stochastic design.

Collision and clearance rejections belong in **adversarial acceptance fixtures**, not a deliberately hazardous policy comparison case. Use a synthetic obstacle/vehicle placement with exactly `0.25` margin and another `0.125` margin to verify the local filter and external monitor separately; a policy run that attempts the latter is labeled a rejection, not an observed collision. This keeps invalid initial geometry out of the comparison denominator.

## Interfaces, audit and acceptance tests to plan

Proposed API shape (names can be refined in implementation): `observe(scenario_static, own_state, other_visible_state, own_history) -> Observation`; `decide(observation, private_state) -> (bounded_action, next_private_state, reason)`; `local_filter(observation, candidate) -> allowed/cause`; `step` gathers both actions simultaneously and passes their joint result to the external monitor. An instance is bound to a vehicle ID and gets only its own history. A trace records each observation's visible fields, policy ID/version, private phase, selected candidate, filter cause, accepted/rejected joint step and goal time. Do not log hidden other-policy state into a controller input.

Acceptance tests before any result table:

- Schema rejects absent/invalid goal, gate, speed limit or impossible solo bay route; v0.1 replay remains readable or a schema version migration is explicit.
- The same scenario and policy produce byte-identical traces, and swapping policy labels alone does not change geometry, observations or motion limits.
- An adversarial controller cannot access the other controller's private state or the simulator's proposed joint step; communication-free policies receive no message channel.
- Exact speed cap, axis-only motion, held heading/footprint, boundary, obstacle and vehicle swept clearance checks; same local filter implementation for every policy. Global monitor independently rejects an adversarial tunneling/collision proposal and retains the last accepted state.
- Fixed priority and first-arrival cases include reversed order and same-tick tie; arrival history is reconstructed from local observations, with disagreement detected.
- Constructed success, collision rejection, clearance rejection, boundary/invalid rejection, deadlock and timeout each have the declared terminal label and precedence. A moving oscillation times out; a finished car does not cause a false deadlock.
- Goal times and delay arithmetic match hand-calculated small traces; failures leave undefined completion times; paired mirrored cases report direction-specific outcomes.

## Implementation sequence after design approval

1. Freeze the experiment contract and scenario schema, including feasibility and solo reference rules; review numeric geometry with a diagram and hand-worked routes.
2. Separate simulator orchestration, independent controller observations and global audit. Add bounded motion and goal semantics while retaining v0.1 scripted mode for replay compatibility.
3. Add the common waypoint planner/local filter and prove its behavior on synthetic geometry tests. Then implement the three exact policies without policy-specific safety exceptions.
4. Build and validate the manifest, traces, outcome classifier and metrics. Run all policies over the frozen suite; publish raw tables/traces within the repo as **descriptive baseline evidence** only, with tests and CI.
5. Review anomalous cases and limitations before owner acceptance. v0.3 protocol, v0.4 robustness/statistical experiments and v0.5 manuscript remain separate design decisions.

## Owner decisions requested

1. Approve or revise the proposed **abstract translation model** as the basis of v0.2 comparison. If physical steering feasibility is a near-term requirement, choose a separate motion-model study first.
2. Approve or revise the **full-visibility, zero-latency observation assumption** for baseline comparisons. It simplifies the first-arrival rule and later needs an occlusion/latency sensitivity study.
3. Confirm whether first-arrival is required in v0.2 as a third baseline, or should remain an optional comparator. The fixed-direction and independent policies are the minimum research-question baselines.
